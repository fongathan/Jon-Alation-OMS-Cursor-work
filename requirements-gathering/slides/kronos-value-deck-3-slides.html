What Kronos is — and what it does for us

Slide 1 of 3

Definitions

Sustainable inventory — A governed register of what data-related assets exist in the business: third-party vendors, digital trackers, internal systems, and the business use cases that connect them. Inventory is the prerequisite for privacy, security, and records obligations — you cannot protect or retire what you cannot name.

Kronos (program) — Disney Streaming’s initiative to move from spreadsheet- and tribal-knowledge inventories to a single, searchable system of record for sustainable inventory management. The name reflects time-based lifecycle: register, attest, review, and deboard assets on cadence.

Kronos UI (POC) — A stakeholder-facing experience for discovery, relationship mapping, registration workflows, audit exports, and legal-exception tracking — designed to sit on a governed store (e.g. SISU / PostgreSQL or Snowflake-backed inventory tables).

Program goal: One place to answer “What vendors, trackers, and systems touch guest data — and who owns them?” without opening five Excel files or Slack threads.

What it does for the organization

  • Unified asset types — Vendors, digital trackers, systems, and business use cases in one model with shared fields (owner, risk, DPA status, region, lifecycle state).
  • Search & discovery — Cross-inventory search so privacy, marketing, and engineering teams find assets by name, owner, or data shared — not org chart guesswork.
  • Relationship graph — See how vendors connect to trackers, systems, and use cases; support impact analysis when contracts change or policies update.
  • Register & deboard — Intake path for new assets and formal decommissioning — reducing “ghost” trackers and orphaned vendor relationships.
  • Reports & audit exports — Vendor, tracker, and full-inventory CSV exports for governance reviews, RIM attestation, and audit evidence.
  • Legal exceptions — Track approved deviations from standard policy with owner, expiry, and review status — visible alongside the inventory, not in email.
  • Bridge to enterprise governance — Inventory feeds privacy remediation (trackers), vendor risk (DPAs), and records management (RIM / disposition) programs.

Architecture: Inventory store as system of record; Kronos UI as the operational face for stewards and program owners. Deep links to specialist tools (DSAR, consent, RIM) where execution stays authoritative.

Why it matters — privacy, compliance, and operational risk

Slide 2 of 3

Why it matters now

  • Tracker and vendor sprawl — Marketing, product, and analytics teams onboard tools faster than central inventories update — creating blind spots for consent, DSAR, and security review.
  • Privacy regulations expect inventory — CCPA, GDPR, and internal GIS standards require knowing what collects data, why, and who is accountable — not after an incident.
  • Remediation depends on a source list — Tracker remediation, vendor DPAs, and access reviews all need the same asset register; duplicate spreadsheets diverge within weeks.
  • Records & retention — RIM programs (legal holds, disposition, attestation) require linked inventory context — which systems and vendors hold which record classes.

Data Governance benefits

  • Single inventory truth — Federated stewardship with domain owners updating their assets on cadence.
  • Risk visibility — Vendor risk ratings, DPA status, and tracker consent posture surfaced at discovery time.
  • Audit-ready exports — Repeatable reports for internal audit, privacy assessments, and regulatory inquiry prep.
  • Lifecycle discipline — Register → active → under review → deprecated/deboarded — reduces “zombie” assets.

Benefits for the whole org

  • Privacy & legal — Complete tracker and vendor landscape for consent programs, DSAR scoping, and remediation prioritization.
  • Marketing & product — Self-service lookup before launching a new pixel or SDK; fewer emergency “is this approved?” escalations.
  • Security & GIS — Vendor inventory with data-shared fields and contract expiry supports third-party risk and access reviews.
  • Engineering & platform — Systems registry links technical assets to business use cases — clearer ownership when pipelines or apps change.
  • Records management — Foundation for Kronos RIM workflows (holds, disposition queues, attestation) referenced from the Governance Portal.
  • Leadership — Portfolio view of inventory coverage, open legal exceptions, and remediation backlog — not anecdotal status in slide decks.
FY27 framing: Kronos is the supply-side inventory layer that feeds catalog migration (Navigator/OMS), privacy operations, and compliance posture — sustainable inventory is a named enabler for governed data use, not a one-time audit exercise.

Value now — value at scale — and how we measure success

Slide 3 of 3

Foundation & near-term

  • Inventory baseline — Import and reconcile Disney Streaming inventories (vendors, trackers, systems, use cases) from legacy Excel sources into a governed model.
  • Kronos UI POC — Dashboard, search, detail views, relationship graph, and export paths validated with stewards and privacy partners.
  • Registration workflow — JIRA / intake handoff for net-new assets until full self-service registration is production-ready.
  • Legal exceptions module — Track approved policy deviations with owner and review dates alongside core inventory.
  • Cross-program alignment — Shared asset IDs and fields consumed by tracker remediation and Governance Portal RIM views.

Value at scale

  • System of record — PostgreSQL / Snowflake-backed inventory with API access for downstream privacy and catalog tools.
  • Automated freshness — Connectors from contract systems, tag managers, and CMDB reduce manual spreadsheet drift.
  • Attestation cadences — Domain owners confirm inventory accuracy on schedule; exceptions escalate to governance queue.
  • Enterprise expansion — Extend beyond Disney Streaming to additional segments with shared taxonomy and federated ownership.
  • Integrated remediation — Tracker findings, vendor reviews, and RIM disposition queues pre-filtered from live inventory — not static exports.

How we measure success

  • Coverage — % of in-scope vendors, trackers, and systems registered with owner, risk tier, and lifecycle status.
  • Freshness — % of assets attested within the last review cycle; count of assets past due for review.
  • Discovery efficiency — Reduction in ad hoc “what trackers do we use?” requests to governance / privacy teams.
  • Remediation linkage — % of open tracker findings mapped to a registered inventory asset.
  • Deboarding discipline — Time from deprecation decision to inventory status update and contract/tool removal.
  • Audit readiness — Successful export of full inventory for privacy / RIM audits without manual consolidation.
Phasing: Baseline + POC → governed store + attestation → automation + enterprise expansion → federated execution with Governance Portal and Navigator.
